# Masterminds — reviewed business profile

This file contains reviewed business facts, policies, goals, and open questions. Gabriel's personal identity and preferences belong in `gabriel.md`.

## Mission and current position

- Masterminds is an ASVAB tutoring company in Puerto Rico.
- Its goal is not merely to produce passing scores. It seeks to maximize each student's score so the student has access to better-paid military jobs and a stronger future.
- Masterminds earned approximately $200,000 last year according to Gabriel and is experiencing rapid student growth.
- Gabriel reported that, in a recent testing cohort, all four Masterminds students passed while the other 32 test-takers did not. The Masterminds students received AFQT percentile scores of 99, 92, 84, and 74.

## Payment and enrollment

- Payment and installment operations are largely manual.
- A recent flow was orientation, student payment through ATH Móvil, salesperson spreadsheet entry, and required documents still pending.
- Payment alone does not activate enrollment. Enrollment remains pending until all required documents are signed.
- Email may support completion reminders. Telegram is a parked future communication adapter, not the owner of enrollment policy.

## Teaching model

- Students are approximately 17–38 years old and arrive with widely varying educational foundations.
- Basic Math is taught on Wednesdays by an instructor other than Gabriel. It covers foundations such as order of operations, exponents, fractions, percentages, addition, and subtraction.
- Passing the Basic Math exam permits entry into Regular Math.
- Gabriel teaches Regular Math on Tuesdays and Thursdays.
- Regular Math is a cyclical survey of ASVAB topics. Current examples include geometry, composite figures, slope-intercept form, basic algebra, simple probability, and word problems.
- Calculators are not allowed during the mathematics work for which Masterminds prepares students. Slow multiplication and division—especially with decimals—and weak fraction understanding are common constraints.

## Curriculum and progression policy

- Students enter Regular Math immediately after passing Basic Math, regardless of the cycle's current position.
- Passing Basic Math permits entry but does not guarantee readiness for the current Regular Math topic.
- Keeping students present in motivating live classes takes priority over delaying entry until every prerequisite is complete.
- Missing prerequisites should be addressed through parallel, just-in-time support.
- The current topic cycle exists but is not yet a sufficiently explicit or fine-tuned core specification.
- Masterminds needs an authoritative mathematics topic inventory, prerequisite map, teaching order, and repeatable cycle.

## Assessment and readiness

- Masterminds is testing-heavy: students receive per-topic exams and monthly simulated ASVABs.
- Students who took a recent official ASVAB reported that the Masterminds simulation was harder than the official exam. Treat this as student-reported evidence, not a calibrated difficulty measurement.
- Student reporting should show performance and improvement over time by ASVAB domain, not only the latest total score.
- Masterminds aims to send students to the official ASVAB only when evidence indicates that they are ready.
- The ASVAB Group is the final-readiness process. It coordinates recruiter contact, exam dates, paperwork, and repeated intensive testing before the official exam.
- Current ASVAB Group readiness considers academic performance, physical readiness, and observable responsibility.
- Responsibility evidence includes consistent class attendance, completing assigned work and exams, attending boot camps, following instructions, and not causing problems there.
- In-person boot camps include exercise drills and an obstacle course, sometimes supported by Army drill sergeants. They are both a readiness activity and a major differentiator from competitors.
- Physical readiness uses boot-camp observations, applicable body-composition requirements, and existing Initial Strength Test logic.

## Product direction

- A student-facing ASVAB Almanac should make the current curriculum-cycle position transparent to every student.
- It should provide foundational resources, formulas, worked material, and catch-up recommendations connected to the current topic.
- Recommendations should eventually be personalized using observed exam gaps and domain-level performance rather than only the current cycle position.
- Teaching content, business policy, and software implementation should remain explicit domains. They may share reviewed facts, but specialized teaching behavior should not become global software-agent behavior.
